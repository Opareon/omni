Omni Editor

20% estimated

An editor that turns your own video or podcast audio into a finished, polished video by itself.

Estimated progress: 20% of the full editor. This is an estimate from the milestone weights below, not a measure of finished code. None of the intelligent editing is built yet: what exists is the foundation, which has now run on your Mac, and an interface, which hasn't been checked there.

The dream

  • Input: your own video, or podcast audio.
  • Understanding: it reads the transcript and listens to the audio for what you are saying and where the strong moments and pauses are.
  • Cuts: it decides where to cut.
  • Visuals: it picks fitting reference images and paintings you have approved.
  • Motion: it adds animations, orbit-like effects and motion graphics that match what you are saying.
  • Storyboard selector: before the final video, it lays the edit out as a storyboard in sections. You see each section's progress, and you review and approve the sections one by one.
  • Output: once the sections are approved, it renders a polished video you can play, automatically.

Where it stands

  • Built: the Video Pipeline foundation. It copies the video so the original is never touched, transcribes it with Whisper, makes a captions file, evens out the loudness and exports an MP4. It makes no cuts, no reordering, no music and no effects.
  • Verified on your Mac (November 15): the foundation. IMG_2780 came out as "IMG_2780 - edited.mp4", 1080x1920 H.264 with AAC audio, plus an .srt captions file, and a full decode passed. Its length and caption text were not checked.
  • Built: the Kintsugi interface, with Import, Jobs and Details panels. It is the window around the foundation, not editing ability. It has been tested only on Sesame's test machine, not on your Mac.
  • Captions in the picture: your Mac's Homebrew FFmpeg 9.0.2 has no libass, so it can't burn captions into the picture. Captions will be drawn with HyperFrames instead.
  • HyperFrames: Node 26.13.1 on your Mac meets its requirement, but a new render hasn't been proven.
  • Next: whispermlx, for a timestamp on every word. It is not installed yet.
  • Not built: reading meaning from the transcript, deciding cuts, choosing images and paintings, animations and effects, the storyboard selector with section-by-section review, and automatic rendering of an edited video. The interface's Jobs panel shows progress for each video, not for each section of an edit.
  • Draft, not approved: the Blue Seam editing style. It is not in use and is not a settled requirement.

How we work

  • Storyboard first, always: every clip is broken into parts and shown as a storyboard before anything renders.
  • One command per stage: each stage becomes a ready-made script in GitHub, so Pi runs one command. Gemma, the model Pi runs, is slow at reasoning through many steps.

Project direction

The five-year vision comes first

The first three years are for defining who I am and focusing on myself: "I'm selfishly focused on myself." The next two are for creating space for other people through my art.

  • Years 1 to 3: build the car and establish my footprint, starting in Atlanta. Develop and share my paintings and music, and build the systems that support being fully seen.
  • Years 4 to 5: turn the established work and platform toward philanthropic art and cultural diplomacy, showcasing people's humanity across borders and connecting divided communities and countries.

Omni

Omni is Kobe's name for the overall application and digital virtual assistant, starting with editing and distribution needs. Ollama is its planned local model-serving component, described in Kobe's own metaphor as Omni's "composure system." Claude Code is the tool for building the application.

Build a workflow that accepts video files, studies reference videos, creates a brand sheet and presents a storyboard for review before editing. After Kobe approves the finished edit, connect it to publishing across his chosen platforms.
