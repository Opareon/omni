Limited print launch: the 90-day runway

Saved Nov 3, 2026, 11:34am

Revised timing: November and December 2026 are for building the components. The ninety-day content dash and its parallel print runway start in January 2027, with the exact January start date still open. Preserve the existing relative milestones, including sales after Day 90.

Run your 13-Sunday content plan as it stands for 90 days. Build Platinum Tsunami as the first limited print beside it, so the reveal lands at the close and sales open right after Day 90.

Days are relative. Day 1 is the Sunday the series intro goes up, as in your content plan. The start month is January 2027; no exact start date has been selected or scheduled.

Workload: the content plan doesn't change. The launch adds about one admin block a week, and the only new filming is the reveal shots on film day 3.

Before Day 1: set the foundation

  • First limited print confirmed: Platinum Tsunami. Build the release package around this painting.
  • Fix opareon.com without the www. It still shows an old Squarespace page and a security warning. Your pins will send people to your site, so fix this first.
  • Check your print files. Make sure you have a high-resolution capture of Platinum Tsunami that's good enough for cotton paper.
  • Film day 1 as planned. The series intro, The Covenant and Platinum Tsunami. Nothing extra here.
  • Start a simple interest list. Anyone who DMs or comments about owning a piece goes on it. This becomes your release audience.

Confirmed first print: Platinum Tsunami

Platinum Tsunami leads the limited-print release. Its Sunday videos run Days 22 to 35. Print results will inform the sculpture shortlist, not prove sculpture demand.

Days 1 to 35: lock the package

On screen: the intro, The Covenant (Days 8 to 21) and Platinum Tsunami (Days 22 to 35). No selling yet: soft invitations only, as the content plan says.

  • Edition size, by Day 35. It decides how many products you build on the site, and whether to sell one number at a time or in rounds.
  • Price structure, by Day 35. The same price for every number, or rising with each number, as in your app notes (number 1 cheapest). The starting price isn't set.
  • Edition name, by Day 35. Platinum Edition, Premium Edition, or something else.
  • Cotton-paper printing and framing quotes, by about Day 21. Who prints, who frames, cost per piece and turnaround. It has to arrive ready to hang.
  • Metal card quotes, by about Day 21. The electroplated card with NFC or QR. Decide whether to make it or have it made, and check lead times.
  • Order samples, by Day 35. One proof print, one frame and one card prototype, so they're in hand before film day 3.
  • Write down where things stand. Every two weeks, note DMs, shares, saves and Pinterest clicks, as in the content plan. This is your before-release baseline.

Days 36 to 77: prove the package

On screen, in order: Blue Boy Holding Space, Blue Boy Rose Tinted Glasses and Seen (the fifth painting, spelled S-E-E-N). Film day 2 falls around Days 28 to 33, and film day 3 around Days 58 to 62.

  • Let the collector clip show the making. The collector-experience clip already on Day 13 of each block can show the proof print, the frame or the card. Show it, don't explain it.
  • Approve the proof, by about Day 50. Check the color, paper, frame and card together, as the collector will get them.
  • Decide on AR, by about Day 56. The app isn't built yet. Decide whether AR is in this first release. Your reveal can only show what really comes in the box.
  • Film the reveal on film day 3. The framed work, the card and AR (if it's ready), all together, at your uncle's home. This is your "show, don't tell" announcement.
  • Plan how it ships, by about Day 70. Packing a framed piece, the edition number on the card and certificate, and what the box looks like when it's opened.
  • Build the product page out of sight, by Day 77. Limited editions go in your Originals, Limited Editions and Standard Products store, not the print store. Test the one-number-at-a-time code there before anyone sees it.

If AR isn't ready

  • Release without AR: the print, frame and card ship now, and you leave AR out of the reveal.
  • Card links to your site for now: the NFC or QR opens the piece's page until the app exists.
  • Hold the release: slide everything until AR works. The days are relative, so nothing breaks.

Days 78 to 90: the reveal

On screen: the three Blue Boy pieces together (Days 78 to 84), then the closing film (Day 85).

  • Grow the list. The content plan already invites people to "join my list to hear first when the Blue Boy works are shown." Point the same invitation at the limited edition.
  • Reveal around Day 85. Cut the reveal footage into the closing film, or post it as its own clip that week. Make it clear everything shown is included.
  • Tell your list first. Give them the release day and how it works before you post it publicly.
  • Final check. Go through the readiness list below. If anything's missing, slide the release, not the content.

Day 91 on: release and measure

  • Open sales. One number at a time on opareon.com, if you choose the method that already works on your site. Bidding through the app is for later.
  • Keep the Sundays going. Behind-the-scenes of packing and shipping, with buyers' permission, keeps the momentum going.
  • Thank every buyer personally. Ask where they first saw the piece. That one question tells you which content sells.

What to track for about four weeks

  • Sell-through: how many numbers sold, and how fast.
  • Price response: whether sales slowed as the numbers rose, if prices rise.
  • Source: which painting, video or platform each buyer came from.
  • List to buyer: how many people on your list bought.
  • The package: whether people used the card and AR, and whether anything arrived damaged.

After: sculptures wait for the results

Strong print sales say people want that image. They don't prove anyone will pay sculpture prices.

  • Use the results to shortlist. Platinum Tsunami is already your first sculpture. The results can confirm it or point to a Blue Boy sculpture instead.
  • Test sculpture demand on its own. Show a prototype from your own clay and plating tests, then measure interest in the sculpture itself before you make a run of ten.
  • Auction access stays open. App-only, invite-only and tiered are still options, not rules.
