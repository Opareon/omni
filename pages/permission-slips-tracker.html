01. You have permission to be creative.Draft

“The whole goal is to explore. The whole goal is to let loose.”

“It is your birthright as a human being to be creative.”

“Tap into it within yourself.”

Story to develop: You were still carrying the rule that black was not allowed in the palette. Mixing black with red gave you the deep, chromatic shadows that made your paintings pop.

Creative direction: Put creativity at the forefront, prepare the tools, then have fun with the flow rather than being hard on yourself.

Logic is the map

The logic is the map you need, but you don't live in the map. It's the GPS, not the destination.

You need the map to get to the studio, but once you're at the canvas, the map doesn't paint the picture.

02. Trust your perspective.Draft: the cave and the courage to step outside

Supporting narrative: your reflection on Plato's Allegory of the Cave.

Storyline: People sit watching shadows on a wall, not realizing there is more beyond them. Leaving the cave changes what a person can see. Returning to explain that change does not mean the others will believe it.

Your connection: You thought you were blocked, but you needed to flow. The old narrative of productive anxiety said you needed everything figured out before moving. Not knowing is part of the process, not a reason to stop.

“I thought I was blocked, but I just needed to flow.”

“To walk out of the cave is significant.”

“Trust your perspective.”

Draft arc: the familiar shadows, stepping outside, seeing differently, then trusting your perspective without waiting for everyone else to see it too.

And I'm like, that's not me. I'm an artist. I was born to create. I'm fucking good at it.

So that realization changed my brain chemistry to know that I am my biggest cheerleader, my biggest hype man, I'm my biggest everything.

I am the whole team first, and then we allocate those. Pass to other people later, but know that if they're not doing their job, I believe we will be fired, and we will. Put in that place. We have that internal system built.

04. Permission to exist without creating.Draft: breaking the pattern of productivity anxiety

Your story: There are stretches of the creative process when you are not making anything. You might be doom scrolling, lying on the couch, taking a drive, or doing ordinary day-to-day things.

You used to see that time as bad and feel you needed to produce something immediately. You call that pressure “productivity anxiety.”

Spending time with your mom helped you notice a familiar pattern: she could not sit still without cooking, cleaning, or finding something to do with her free time.

You asked how that pattern was showing up in your own creative process. Recognizing it in your life, you chose to stop repeating it and give yourself permission to exist without creating.

“I figured out, like, wait a minute, how is this showing up in mine?”

“I am making that same pattern within my life. So I cut that out.”

“Permission to exist without creating.”

05. Create for creation's sake.Draft: trusting creation and recognizing the record of your progression

Your reflection, captured from this conversation and lightly organized for clarity. Where the audio was unclear, the uncertainty is kept rather than filled in.

What the early work was building

Early in your journey, not getting into galleries or art shows felt like failure. You thought it was your work, rather than considering the timing and the time needed to cultivate what was developing.

You now recognize that your past work was practice for where you are today. It built your skills, your network, your experience, and your connections.

It taught you how to put together a portfolio and, now, how to understand and develop a cohesive body of work. You did not understand all of that while it was happening.

Your current body of work has been transformative. It is your most honest and truthful work to date.

The rabbit hole and your own path

You were experimenting and going down rabbit holes. Now you recognize that this is where the power lies, rather than seeing that exploration as a detour.

You contrasted a stagnant, mundane life of copying and repeating with finding your niche, your exact eye, and your path.

“When the path opens for you and only you, mark that motherfucker.”

You described that path as leading toward abundance, blessings, and growth, and urged yourself to stand in it.

Create for creation's sake. Trust your creation. There are things outside you that can hinder you; listening to that noise will not help you follow what is yours.

“Just listen to your light.”

Listen to what it brings you and where it takes you. Trust that the path can open as you create.

The painting you still do not like

You also spoke about a particular older painting you still do not like, even though it required so much of you. Its title was unclear in the audio, with “Rich Boy” among the words heard; the title is not settled here.

You recalled a beautiful sketch, working in one layer, and underpainting. Parts of your description of the execution were unclear, including whether it felt too perfect or controlled.

What was clear: the painting remains a reminder and a testament to the state of mind you were in at that time. Recognizing its place in your history does not mean you now have to like it.

A historical record, not proof of failure

You described the older work as a historical record of your progression and what you were able to do at each stage. The skills and lessons are part of what you use now.

The older paintings preserve where you were, while the work you make today shows where that practice has brought you. The exploration was part of developing your own eye and voice.

The permission at the heart of this reflection: create for creation's sake, trust your creation, and listen to your own light rather than letting outside noise decide your path.

06. Let the doubt breathe.Draft: softness, self-trust, and liberation in Starboy

While recording your song Starboy, you found the first verse difficult to deliver the way you wanted, even with the words already written. You kept recording it, but felt friction around proclaiming yourself the star.

“Who am I to proclaim that I am the star?”

“Let me rest in my softness.”

The first part feels soft and vulnerable. The later part comes more easily, with a deeper voice and a new energy. You described the phrase as an activation and the later part as reinforcing the first.

The realization you wanted to keep: let the doubt and friction in the first verse sit instead of trying to erase them. Giving that vulnerability room can make the liberation in the second verse land.

This is permission to let the first part breathe, rather than forcing it to sound like the confidence that comes later. The contrast belongs in the song.

“So breathe out, like let it be what it is.”

Recording cue

“Am I sharpening the emotion, or am I trying to edit out the softness?”

07. Put your burdens and your pain on the canvas so you don't have to carry them.Draft: Blue Boy, becoming, and making room to live

“Put your burdens, put your pain on the canvas, so you don't have to carry it.”

Blue Boy has been the permission slips you needed. The series gave you somewhere to express burdens, hurt, and expectations instead of keeping them at the center of your life.

The becoming realization

Today you spoke with your father after months apart. You journaled beforehand, then had the practical conversation about insurance without the same internal emotional upheaval you remembered from earlier versions of yourself.

The difference was between saying you had changed and experiencing that change. You could have a dialogue without giving him the same emotional access. Releasing disappointment did not mean removing your boundaries.

“I'm becoming that I'm becoming, and that becoming is me.”

You recognized how often you had been bracing for a hit. This conversation felt like evidence that you could relate differently, rather than keep rehearsing the old story.

You want that realization in Becoming and The Unbecoming: stepping outside the imposed mold and living as the person you are becoming. The canvas can hold the record without requiring you to stay in that chapter.

The message beyond your own story

Start with what the paintings have done in your life. Then offer viewers a possibility, not a promise: a place to recognize what they carry and consider what they might express, release, or stop carrying alone.

The body metaphor — not a medical explanation

You brought up autoimmune disease as an image of a system turning against itself, connecting it to the experience of carrying pain inward. Keep that clearly as a metaphor, not a claim about what causes illness.

Autoimmune disease involves the immune system mistakenly attacking healthy tissue, not simply breaking down old cells. Carrying emotional burdens is not an established explanation for it, and making art is not a treatment or a guarantee of prevention.

The artistic message can stand without that medical comparison: give pain somewhere to be expressed, so it does not have to define the life you are building.

10. You are free to rewrite your code.Draft: Platinum Tsunami, refinement and recreation

The old story is not the only story

You once felt your old life and old story were all you knew, and therefore who you were. The realization behind this permission slip: you can always recreate yourself.

Your self-help journey took you through books including Think and Grow Rich. Consuming them did not fill the void. The shift came when you began applying what you were learning rather than only ingesting more information.

You want to revisit Think and Grow Rich to see what you have learned since. Its sixth-sense section particularly intrigued you and opened an exploration of your spiritual and metaphysical mind space.

Mapping your mind space

Through meditation and the journey you called Quantum Leap, you intentionally explored the landscape of your mind: colors, feelings, thoughts, their shapes, textures, storage, density, and relationships.

You described this as exploring your code, including what you arrive with and what develops through your experiences. Natal charts and Human Design are among the symbolic languages you use to explore that understanding.

You trust that learning other languages will expand what you can express. You did not have the language then that you have now; maturity and reassurance in your personhood have helped the experience become clearer.

Faith, doubt, and seeing clearly

You want to understand the roots of your beliefs, your biases, and the conditions you were placed in. Tell the story for what it is and see life for what it is, not only what you want it to be.

You described faith as a healthy dose of delusion: enough possibility to empower you to act, without denying facts, harming your reality, or robbing yourself of your actual experience.

Your mustard-seed image is about balance: you do not need to overdose on certainty. Doubt is like salt, sometimes the soup needs it to taste right.

Words matter because they help map the story you live and how you carry yourself. Rewriting your code means exercising that authorship with awareness.

The figure in Platinum Tsunami

The man has rewritten himself into a new state, a new being. Through repeated refinement and recreation, he becomes another vessel, almost cyborg, another form of himself. He is still a man.

In your visual metaphor, platinum embodies that refinement. Fire, pressure, heat, and adversity are the conditions through which he molds and shapes the intricate parts of himself.

The emphasis is on his authorship: he shapes himself rather than remaining confined to the old story. He knows who he is while continuing to become.

“He's also a masterpiece at the same time.”

Being a work in progress and being a masterpiece are not opposites. He is both, now.

11. The world is your bubble.Draft: carrying your sacred space wherever you go

You used to imagine a protective bubble close around you so you could feel safe and comfortable within your own space. Now you can imagine expanding that bubble outward to all existence, even the whole universe.

“No matter where I go, I am safe and rooted within who I am.”

“Everywhere I go is sacred.”

In your studio, you can float, explore, and express your full range. Some people bring out your shyness; others bring out more of your expression. You recognize that you can carry that expressive self beyond the spaces you already find sacred.

Entering another room does not mean leaving yourself behind. It is a space where you are, where your perspective and the way you map things out also exist.

You connected this to scanning a space with your headset: taking in the space around you became an image for occupying it and extending your sense of belonging outward.

You also recognized that your creativity did not have to remain in separate brackets so you could understand it. Allowing yourself to create for creation's sake brought liberation and freedom.
