How the Sunday video works

The Sunday video is the one thing you make each week. Everything else that week is cut from it.

  • Length: aim for 8–15 minutes. The opening episode can be shorter.
  • All four pillars in every episode: theme, painting process, internal shift and viewer takeaway. Part 1 leans on theme and process. Part 2 leans on the shift, the takeaway and the collector side.
  • What goes into the edit: your sit-down at your uncle's home, studio footage from painting you were doing anyway, and a nature segment when you have one.
  • Mark the clips while you edit: one moment for each pillar, plus a few stills or quotes for the carousel. That's the week's short content, already chosen.

Part 1 and Part 2 aren't two films. Each painting still gets one sit-down at your uncle's home, as in the first plan. You record it a little longer and split it across two Sundays.

Part 2 is a continuation: the same sit-down's second half, edited with newer studio and nature footage. The Blue Boy series episode reuses what the three Blue Boy sit-downs didn't use. So 13 Sundays come from 7 recordings, not 13.

Four platforms, four jobs

  • Instagram: Shares sent in DMs are now one of the strongest ranking signals. Original, non-watermarked video gets favored, and Trial Reels let you test a clip with non-followers first. So export clean clips from your edit, not TikTok downloads. Hootsuite
  • TikTok: Reach comes from watch time and finished views, not follower count. People also use it as a search engine, so say "oil painting" and "Black portrait" out loud and put them on screen. Agorapulse
  • YouTube: Shorts bring new viewers, and long videos turn them into people who come back. Link each Short to that week's full video. Videos over eight minutes can carry mid-roll ads. MusicPromoToday
  • Pinterest: It works as a visual search engine. Pins keep sending traffic for months, so link each one to that painting's page on your website. Growth often shows 60 to 90 days in, which lines up with this plan. Frieda Creates
  • Left out: Threads, because it's built for text and conversation more than art sales. An email list isn't one of the four, but a short monthly note to people who've asked about your work belongs to you, not to an algorithm.

Three settings, rotated

  • Your uncle's home: the sit-downs. One per painting on its theme and story, recorded long enough to split into Part 1 and Part 2. Plus the series intro and the closing film.
  • Your studio: behind-the-scenes footage. Brushwork, color and skin-tone mixing, layers, close-ups and working in real time. Keep a camera rolling while you paint anyway. It goes into the Sunday edit, so it's not a separate content shoot.
  • Nature: outside, doing something. Walking while you talk about the inner shift and the takeaway. Record one about every other week when you're already out, mostly for Part 2. It's optional, not a shoot you owe the plan.

The rotation now happens inside each video and across the week's clips: a clip from your uncle's home, then studio, then nature, so two posts in a row rarely look alike.

Weekly rhythm

  • Before Sunday, the edit: put the episode together and mark four clips and the carousel stills. This replaces the separate studio session and talk.
  • Sunday: the full video goes up on YouTube. Point to it in Stories and pin its thumbnail to the painting's page.
  • Monday to Saturday: four clips and one carousel from that video, spread across the week. Each clip goes out as a Reel, a TikTok and a Short.
  • One rest day: Stories only, if anything.
  • Stories most days: loose and unplanned. This is still where the real-life moments go.
  • 10 minutes of replies: answer comments and DMs. Answer good questions with a video reply when you feel like it.

Film days at your uncle's home

  • Film day 1, the week before Day 1: the series intro, The Covenant and Platinum Tsunami. The intro has to exist before Day 1 now, so this shoot moves earlier than in the first plan.
  • Film day 2, around days 28–33: Blue Boy Holding Space and Blue Boy Rose Tinted Glasses. Holding Space Part 1 goes up on Day 36.
  • Film day 3, around days 58–62: Seen and the closing film. If you'd rather reflect on the 90 days closer to the end, record the closing talk outside around Day 82 instead.
  • Each painting's sit-down: aim for 20–30 minutes of good material, enough for two 8–15 minute episodes once studio footage is cut in.

Pillars and angles for each painting

These are your four pillars (theme, painting process, internal shift, viewer takeaway), plus a fifth I'd add: collector experience. That's how a piece lives with someone, like the hidden writing revealed by NFC tag, AR, or a studio visit.

Every Sunday episode touches all four pillars. Part 1 leans on theme and process, and Part 2 on the shift, the takeaway and the collector side.

Opening, days 1–7

  • Sunday, series intro video: why these five paintings, and why now, from film day 1, with teaser close-ups cut in.
  • Clips from it: the "why now" moment, and one close-up from each painting, with no full reveals yet.
  • Teaser carousel: stills of those close-ups.
  • Set up Pinterest: one board per painting plus a Blue Boy series board, and a page on your website for each painting.

The Covenant, days 8–21

  • Theme (suggested): a covenant with yourself, which ties to self-ownership. Prompt: "Who is this covenant with?"
  • Process: close-ups of each layer, and deep skin-tone mixing on camera.
  • Internal shift: what you promised yourself while painting it, ideally from a nature segment in Part 2.
  • Takeaway question: "What promise are you keeping to yourself?"
  • Collector: the hidden-writing reveal, if this piece has one.

Platinum Tsunami, days 22–35

  • Theme (suggested): radiant presence and power that doesn't shrink.
  • Process: the hardest passage to paint, start to finish, in close-up.
  • Internal shift: moving from survival to strategy, and abundance.
  • Takeaway question: "What wave are you still holding back?"
  • Collector: a carousel of close-ups you only notice in person.

Blue Boy Holding Space, days 36–49

  • Theme (suggested): tenderness as part of Black masculinity.
  • Process: portrait realism, the face from first layer to last.
  • Internal shift: softness, rest and boundaries.
  • Takeaway question: "Who holds space for you, and for whom do you?"
  • Collector: what an AR or NFC moment adds when you stand in front of the piece.

Blue Boy Rose Tinted Glasses, days 50–63

  • Theme (suggested): how we choose to see, and when hope turns into denial.
  • Process: the last layers and the finishing details.
  • Internal shift: seeing yourself clearly.
  • Takeaway question: "What are you still seeing through rose-tinted glasses?"
  • Collector: the hidden writing or AR moment, if this piece has one.

Seen, fifth painting, days 64–77

  • Theme (suggested): being seen versus being looked at, and representation.
  • Process: from your reference to the canvas, and the choices you made along the way.
  • Internal shift: letting yourself be visible without apologizing.
  • Takeaway question: "Where do you feel truly seen?"
  • Collector: where this piece sits within the Blue Boy series.

Blue Boy, all three together, days 78–84

  • Sunday, series episode (a continuation, not a new painting): the three Blue Boy pieces side by side, edited from what their three sit-downs didn't use, plus studio footage.
  • Optional new segment: a short nature talk on what the series says as a whole.
  • Collector: a carousel of the three Blue Boy pieces shown together.
  • Invitation: "Join my list to hear first when the Blue Boy works are shown."

Close, days 85–90

  • Sunday, closing film: all five paintings, and what the 90 days changed in you.
  • Clips from it: one moment for each painting, as time allows.
  • Carousel of all five: one line about each.
  • Open invitation: ask people to message you about any of the works.

Sample week: The Covenant, Part 2 (days 15–21)

What you actually make

The unique work is 7 recordings at your uncle's home, edited into 13 Sunday videos. The rest is those videos cut down and adapted, about 235 posts in all.

Unique work

  • 7 sit-down recordings over three film days: the series intro, five painting sit-downs and the closing film. That's one more than the first plan, for the intro.
  • Studio footage: from painting you're doing anyway. No extra sessions.
  • About 6 nature segments, optional: roughly every other week, when you're already outside.
  • 13 Sunday videos to edit: 1 series intro, 5 Part 1 films, 5 Part 2 continuations, 1 Blue Boy series episode and 1 closing film.

Cut and adapted from the Sunday videos

  • About 50 short clips: four a week, with fewer in the closing week.
  • About 150 short-video posts: each clip goes out as a Reel, a TikTok and a YouTube Short.
  • 13 carousels: one a week, from stills and quotes in the video.
  • About 60 pins: two or three from each carousel, one for each Sunday video, and three at each painting's reveal.
  • Week to week: 1 full YouTube video, 4 Shorts, 4 TikToks, 4 Reels and a carousel on Instagram, and 4 to 5 pins.

Light weeks and getting back on track

  • Minimum week: a shorter Sunday video, even 5 minutes of real-time studio footage with a voiceover, and two clips from it on all three video apps.
  • No time to edit: post clips you marked but didn't use, or repost a close-up as a still with a new caption.
  • Stories only: keeps you present without making anything new.
  • Pause and slide: the days are relative, so a missed Sunday moves the plan instead of breaking it.
  • Post when inspired: the rhythm is a floor, not a quota. Extra posts are welcome when the energy's there.

Soft invitations for collectors

None of these say a piece is for sale. They just open a conversation.

  • "If this one speaks to you, message me. I love hearing what people see in it."
  • "Want to see it in person? Send me a note."
  • "Collectors get a part of this piece the screen can't show." (Only for a piece with hidden writing or AR.)
  • "Join my list to hear first when the Blue Boy works are shown."

How to tell it's working

Check every two weeks. Watch for connection, not follower count.

  • DMs and inquiries about a specific painting.
  • Comments that answer the takeaway question in real words.
  • Sends and shares on Instagram, and saves on carousels.
  • Clicks from Shorts to the full Sunday video, which shows which clips pull people in.
  • Outbound clicks from Pinterest to your painting pages.
  • Returning viewers and average view time on the Sunday videos.
  • On day 90: which painting started the most conversations, and which setting felt most like you.
