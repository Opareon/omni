Your music and art playbook: building the team behind the work

Saved Nov 14, 2026, 1:26am

You don't need a full team yet. Build it in rings: a kit that makes saying yes easy, small paid pilots that prove who follows through, then a stake only for the people who earned it.

Phases and dates below are suggestions, not commitments. Art and music run as separate lanes; they share only the Opareon story and the back office.

How the team actually forms

  • Ring 1: you plus systems. Tracking calls, drafting applications and outreach, keeping the tracker. I can carry this now, so your energy goes to making.
  • Ring 2: paid specialists, per project. Mix engineer, photographer or videographer, stylist, a publicist for one campaign. Clear scope, clear fee, clear end date.
  • Ring 3: people with a stake. Manager, studio manager, advisor, gallery, patron. Only people who already delivered in Ring 2, or who arrive with written terms.
  • The rule that protects you: you keep ownership of the paintings, the masters, the AR and the Opareon name. Collaborators earn credit, fees and agreed shares, not your IP.

1. The kit: make yes easy

Backers move faster when they can see the whole thing in two minutes. Build one kit per lane; don't mix them.

Art kit

  • 9 strong images of work from the last 2 years. That's exactly MOCA GA's application checklist, so build it once and reuse it everywhere.
  • Artist statement and CV. You have the statement; the CV lists your five-plus years of group shows and your publication.
  • A 30 to 60 second AR demo video. The hidden writing appearing is the moment people remember. Shoot it at Articulate ATL.
  • Seen print offer sheet. Edition, size, paper, price. Your plan of 30 at $350 is a projection until preorders say otherwise.
  • Press folder. Every clipping, interview and review. MOCA wants exactly three, so this folder is a scoreboard.

Music kit

  • Starboy one-sheet. Kobe and King in two lines: the soft, grounded voice and the assertive one, and why both stay.
  • Private listening link and a split sheet. Who wrote what, agreed in writing before release.
  • Three press photos and a music bio. Separate from your painter bio, so music people meet the musician.

Shared

  • One Opareon paragraph. For investors and partners who "see the ecosystem": what it connects, and what support would let it move.

2. Art lane: Blue Boy to a solo show

  • The target: MOCA GA's Working Artist Project. $15,000, a solo show at MOCA GA, a catalogue and a studio apprentice. Douglas and Fulton counties both qualify. mocaga.org
  • Your two gaps. At least one major solo show that wasn't part of a degree, and exactly three publications or reviews. You have one.
  • Their timing. The 2026 deadline was June 7. A similar June deadline in 2027 is likely, but not posted yet.
  • Use Articulate ATL as raw material. Install photos, the AR video, the interview, and a list of everyone who asked about price or wanted to stay in touch.
  • Go to the Southern Prize reception Nov 19. South Arts' state fellows are shown at Hambidge Hive, 575 Morosgo Dr NE. It's your field, two days before your show. hambidge.org
  • Press, publication two. At ArtsATL, Senior Editor Danielle Charbonneau takes visual arts releases; there's also a self-serve calendar. Pitch the Blue Boy story, not just a date. artsatl.org
  • Prints: test before you order. Open a Seen preorder or interest list first, and order the edition when the list supports it.
  • The advisor seat. JP is a fit for an unpaid advisor seat: a monthly call, honest feedback, introductions. No money and no stake asked either way.

3. Music lane: Starboy as the pilot release

  • Rights before release, in this order. Distributor, then one PRO (ASCAP or BMI), then The MLC, then SoundExchange. Keep the splits identical everywhere. onestowatch.com
  • The MLC: register as a self-administered songwriter. It's free, takes no commission, and claims your US streaming mechanicals. themlc.com
  • SoundExchange: register twice. As featured performer and as owner of the recording. It's free and pays from Pandora and SiriusXM-type radio. Check your distributor's publishing add-on first, to avoid double claims.
  • Mix pilot on SoundBetter. Post a free brief, hear samples, check credits and reviews. Payment sits in escrow until you're satisfied. soundbetter.com
  • The brief protects Kobe's softness. Say plainly that the soft verse is meant to stay intimate and close, and the contrast with King is the point. Hire whoever gets that on the first pass.
  • Find your music people. AIMP's Atlanta chapter runs industry events; this year's summit had a panel called "When Do You Need a Manager?" aimp.org
  • Grammy U, $50 a year. For emerging music professionals 18 and up, no college needed, with Atlanta Chapter events. Voting membership needs 12 credits, so later. grammy.com
  • Music press. At ArtsATL, Executive Editor Shane Harrison takes music releases. Pitch the release separately from the paintings.

Green flags

  • They ask about your vision before pitching theirs. And they repeat it back accurately.
  • They deliver something small on time. Follow-through on a small job is the only proof that counts.
  • They put scope in writing without being asked. And they've done work close to yours.

Red flags

  • Ownership for "exposure." Anyone who wants your masters, paintings or AR in exchange for visibility.
  • Upfront fees for placements or guaranteed press. Vague dates, no references, pressure to sign fast.
  • They want to fix the softness. If they treat the Kobe voice as a flaw, they don't get the work.

Fair terms for every pilot

  • Scope and date. Exactly what's delivered, in what format, by when.
  • Fee or trade, in writing. Including how many revision rounds, and what extra rounds cost.
  • Ownership and credit. You own the work; they get credit and portfolio use. Music splits go on the split sheet.
  • The exit. Either side can end it with notice, and work done gets paid. No one is locked in.
  • Get it checked for free. Georgia Lawyers for the Arts runs monthly clinics with free 30-minute sessions for members on IP and simple contracts, plus pro bono referrals if your income qualifies. glarts.org

5. Money routes, timed to your breaks

Most 2026 cycles have closed. The next dates below follow each program's 2026 pattern, so check each site before you plan around one.

  • Hambidge residency, opens Dec 1. The summer session (June to August) takes applications Dec 1 to Jan 15. Visual art and music both qualify, and it fits your summer break. hambidge.org
  • Hambidge costs. $300 a week and a $30 application fee. Distinguished Fellowships waive two weeks of fees and add a $700 stipend.
  • South Arts State Fellowship, about February to March. $5,000 for one Georgia artist; the Southern Prize is $25,000. Painting and an experimental category. The 2026 deadline was March 18. southarts.org
  • Idea Capital, about September to November. $1,000 or $2,500, plus a $5,000 award for experimental painting after five years in metro Atlanta. It funds new projects and artist fees, not tech development. ideacapitalatlanta.org
  • Artadia Atlanta, about September. Unrestricted money for visual artists in Douglas, Fulton and nearby counties. The 2026 deadline was Sept 18. artadia.org
  • Check your grad program first. South Arts excludes students in fine arts degrees, and Artadia excludes art-related degrees. An education degree may be fine, but confirm before you apply.
  • Fractured Atlas, for the public AR show. Fiscal sponsorship lets donors give tax-deductible gifts to a noncommercial project: 8% fee plus $10 a month membership. Not for prints. fracturedatlas.org

Outreach you can send

  • Mix engineer: "I'm releasing Starboy, a two-voice record: one soft and grounded, one assertive. I want a paid one-song test. The soft verse has to stay intimate. Can you send two mixes closest to that?"
  • Photographer: "I'm showing Blue Boy paintings with hidden AR writing at Articulate ATL on Nov 21. I'm looking for one documentation shoot, paid or credited, with usage terms agreed first."
  • Advisor (JP): "I'm building Opareon, with painting, AR and music as separate lanes. I'd value a monthly 30-minute call: honest feedback and the occasional intro. Nothing financial attached."
  • ArtsATL visual arts: "Blue Boy is a series on the tenderness of Black men, with affirmations painted over and revealed through AR. Showing Nov 21 in Buckhead. Images and an AR demo are ready."

6. Rhythm and what counts as progress

  • One ops hour a week. Send one ask, improve one asset, log what came back. I can prep the hour so you just decide and send.
  • Breaks are for production and applications. Winter, spring and summer breaks hold the deadlines; weekdays protect your energy.
  • Count follow-through, not followers. Collaborators who delivered on time, and how many you'd hire again.
  • Count the MOCA scoreboard. Publications (now 1 of 3), solo show booked (0 of 1), applications sent and shortlists reached.
  • Count real buyers. Seen preorders against your 30, studio conversations, and people who came back after a show.
  • Count your own time. Hours of making protected each week. If the system eats them, the system changes.

Later: the full team

  • Manager, when the opportunities are real. When admin from actual offers outruns your ops hour, and there's income to share.
  • Gallery or studio manager, after the solo show. Representation follows a body of work people have already seen.
  • Investors in Opareon, on paper. They back the ecosystem with written terms, never the IP itself.

Trusting your own call and asking for real backing aren't opposites. This playbook is how you choose who gets to back you.
