Only Seen’s slip is settled. The other four are proposals. The Covenant’s moved from “Author my own story” to “Let my cracks be the gold seams,” because your notes on it are about the break and the gold repair.

1. The Covenant

Built from: your editor’s notes for The Covenant video: the visual contract with God, the canvas breaking when faith was lost, the gold bond, and “Failure is information.”

  1. Pan, about 10 sec.Shot: slow pan across the whole painting, high-contrast texture, ending on a repaired section.

    “This is The Covenant. A visual contract between me and God.”

  2. Studio, about 20 sec.Shot: close-ups of the repaired sections and the gold bond on the canvas.

    “While I was painting this, the canvas broke. Right when I’d lost faith. I didn’t throw it away. I repaired it, and I bonded it with gold.”

  3. Reflection, about 20 sec.Shot: you at your uncle’s, straight to camera.

    “That break showed me one broken thing can turn into ten new possibilities. The covenant didn’t end when the canvas cracked. It got completed. This one gave me permission to let my cracks be the gold seams.”

  4. Takeaway, about 10 sec.Shot: direct to camera, then fade to black.

    “If something broke on you this year, look at it again. Failure is information. What’s it telling you?”

For the long sit-downPrompts for the 20 to 30 minute talk.
  • Who is this covenant with, and what did making it mean to you?
  • What happened between the break and the repair?
  • What did you choose on the canvas that you carried outside the studio?

2. Platinum Tsunami

Built from: your Radiant Presence note and your sculpture notes (the figure in a durag, with armor). Nothing saved says what the armor means to you, so that line is yours to fill in.

  1. Pan, about 10 sec.Shot: start wide, then hold on the durag and the armor.

    “Platinum Tsunami. No apology on this one.”

  2. Studio, about 15 sec.Shot: the armor coming in, layer by layer, in close-up. If you don’t have early footage, use detail shots of the finished piece.

    “Watch the armor come in, one layer at a time. To me, that armor is .”

  3. Reflection, about 25 sec.Shot: you at your uncle’s.

    “This one is about radiant presence. Simply existing, fully and without apology. It’s not a performance. Stop asking for a seat at the table and realize you are the table. This one gave me permission to stop explaining myself.”

  4. Takeaway, about 10 sec.Shot: straight to camera.

    “No grasping. No proving. So what wave are you still holding back?”

For the long sit-downPrompts for the 20 to 30 minute talk.
  • What does the armor protect, and what would he be without it?
  • Which choice in this painting took trust in your own taste?
  • Why is this the first painting going to sculpture?

3. Blue Boy Holding Space

Built from: your Blue Boy statement and your Holding Space account. The family story stays general, so no one else’s private experience is on camera.

  1. Pan, about 12 sec.Shot: a quiet open on the whole painting, then move to the face.

    “Blue Boy is a Black boy unlearning the burdens the world put on him. The flowers are lessons he learned. The blue is clarity and growth.”

  2. Studio, about 15 sec.Shot: the face, from first layer to last.

    “Holding Space came out of a painful family situation. It wasn’t about me, but I still needed to be there.”

  3. Reflection, about 25 sec.Shot: you at your uncle’s.

    “I found feelings I didn’t know I had. Sadness, grief, disappointment, embarrassment, shame. And I learned I could sit with them without letting them become my whole identity. You can hold space for an emotion without becoming it. This one gave me permission to feel it before I fix it.”

  4. Takeaway, about 10 sec.Shot: straight to camera.

    “Next time somebody shares something painful, pause before you fix it. Ask: would it help if I just listened?”

For the long sit-downPrompts for the 20 to 30 minute talk.
  • What did holding space look like while you painted?
  • What did you let stay tender in the figure?
  • How do you show up for someone without making their pain yours to fix?

4. Blue Boy Rose Tinted Glasses

Built from: your Rose Tinted Glasses account and your caption for its reel.

  1. Pan, about 10 sec.Shot: the full painting, then move to the glasses and the flowers around them.

    “Blue Boy, Rose Tinted Glasses. One of my truest works so far.”

  2. Studio, about 15 sec.Shot: the finishing details, given more room than the reel had.

    “The world has noise. Shiny objects, beautiful things, labels telling me who I’m supposed to be. So the glasses became a filter I chose.”

  3. Reflection, about 25 sec.Shot: you at your uncle’s.

    “I wanted to lock in on joy, peace, abundance and the freedom to be myself. Not pretending pain doesn’t exist. Just not letting the world decide my whole inner experience. Before all the labels, could I just be a human being? This one gave me permission to stop performing the version of me other people wrote.”

  4. Takeaway, about 10 sec.Shot: straight to camera.

    “Not everything beautiful is good for you. Put the feed down and ask: what feels like me when nobody’s telling me who to be?”

For the long sit-downPrompts for the 20 to 30 minute talk.
  • Whose labels were you filtering out?
  • What do the flowers on the glasses hold for you?
  • Where is the line between a chosen filter and denial?

5. Seen

Built from: your Seen account, your note calling it “a declaration of visibility and recognition of self,” and your chosen slip.

  1. Pan, about 10 sec.Shot: the whole composition first, so the full frame makes the point before any detail.

    “Seen. A declaration of visibility. Recognizing myself.”

  2. Studio, about 15 sec.Shot: reference to canvas, and the choices that give the figure presence.

    “For a long time I tried to learn who I was through other people and the world. This painting is what happened when I turned inward.”

  3. Reflection, about 25 sec.Shot: you at your uncle’s.

    “And when I turned inward, I saw someone I loved. I don’t have to do that much to be awesome. My state of being is already awesome. Once my worth stopped depending on what I produce, creating became something I do from love. This one gave me permission to take up the whole frame.”

  4. Takeaway, about 10 sec.Shot: straight to camera, hold the pause at the end.

    “You don’t have to be productive to be worthy of your own love. Take one moment today you don’t turn into content or proof.”

For the long sit-downPrompts for the 20 to 30 minute talk.
  • What’s the difference between being seen and being looked at?
  • What did you let take up room in this work?
  • How does this permission carry into sharing the work and the print?
